01 — The problem

The bin and the empty plate are often a short ride apart.

Waste

1 billion

tonnes of food lost or wasted every year.

Approximately one-fifth to one-third of all food produced globally — amounting to over 1 billion tonnes, or roughly 1 billion meals a day — is lost or wasted every year, costing the global economy roughly $1 trillion annually.

Hunger

Same city

Shelters plan meals without knowing what kitchens still have.

A message that arrives after closing is already too late. Food rescue fails when surplus stays invisible during the only hour it can still be served.

The gap is timing, not goodwill.

Kitchens want a simple way to hand food on. NGOs need portions, a window, and a place to pull up. FoodBridge is that shared view — before anything hits the bin.

02 — The solution

A short, visible bridge between the pass and a community kitchen.

Donors list what is left. Receivers claim what they can serve. Volunteers and NGO vehicles cover the distance while the food is still in its window.

  1. Donor kitchen

    A restaurant, hotel, caterer, or household lists portions, diet notes, and the last sensible pickup time.

  2. FoodBridge

    The board shows what is open nearby, holds a claim, and shares the handoff details with both sides.

  3. Community table

    An NGO, shelter, or campus kitchen receives a count they can actually plate the same night.

03 — How it works

Four steps. Finished before the shutters come down.

  1. 01

    List what is left

    Two minutes at the pass: kitchen name, portions, veg or mixed, and the time the food must leave by.

    About 2 minutes
  2. 02

    Match nearby

    Groups who can receive tonight see the ticket first, with enough detail to say yes or pass.

    Usually under 20 minutes
  3. 03

    Coordinate pickup

    A claim locks the ticket. Entrance notes and a handoff time go to the kitchen and the receiver.

    Before close
  4. 04

    Log the meals

    Once the food is received, the portions count toward the kitchen’s impact — not toward the bin.

    Same night

04 — Features

Made for the last hour of service, not a long form.

Donation

List surplus without a meeting.

Kitchens post what they already know: how many portions, what it is, and when it has to move. No account tour in the middle of cleanup.

NGO partners

Receivers with a real capacity.

Shelters and community kitchens join with the areas and meal sizes they can take tonight.

Pickup

A window, not a vague “ASAP”.

Every ticket names a leave-by time so a volunteer route can be planned while the food is still good.

Community

Volunteers on short loops.

Students and neighbours cover a neighbourhood loop with the entrance, portions, and receiver already on the ticket.

Care

Notes that change a yes.

Veg, mixed, nuts, and packaging sit on the card so a receiving kitchen can decide in one glance.
